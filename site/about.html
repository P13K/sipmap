About SipMap

Find drinking water near you, in one glance.

SipMap is a simple map of public drinking water points. It is made for hikers, trail runners, runners and cyclists who need to refill on the go.

Why SipMap exists

Volunteers around the world have already mapped countless fountains, taps and springs in OpenStreetMap. That data is good, but general-purpose map apps bury it among shops, roads and everything else.

SipMap shows only one thing: where you can drink. Open it, see the water around you, and go.

What you will find on the map

  • Fountains
  • Taps
  • Springs
  • Toilets with drinking water
  • Refill points
  • Wells

Only points that OpenStreetMap marks as drinking water are shown. Taps tagged as non-drinkable and private points are left out.

How it works

  • Every week, SipMap downloads fresh OpenStreetMap data and keeps only the drinking water points.
  • Your location is used only in your browser to sort the nearest points. It is never sent or stored.
  • No account and no cookies.
  • One tap on a point opens a walking route to it in Google Maps.
  • Coverage today: France and Spain, including the Balearic Islands. More countries will follow.

Before you drink

The map is maintained by a community and comes with no guarantee. A fountain can be switched off in winter or out of order. Check any sign on site, and filter or treat spring water if you are unsure.

Spotted a mistake?

Every point links to OpenStreetMap. Fix it there, or add a missing fountain, and SipMap will pick up the change within a week. Everyone who uses OpenStreetMap benefits too.
